Discovering Hidden Visual Concepts Beyond Linguistic Input in Infant Learning

CVPR 2025
Xueyi Ke1, Satoshi Tsutsui1, Yayun Zhang2, Bihan Wen1
1Nanyang Technological University 2The Max Planck Institute for Psycholinguistics
Paper arXiv Code Poster

Abstract

How do infants learn to understand the world around them? This fundamental question drives our research into discovering hidden visual concepts in infant behavior that go beyond what we can easily describe with words.

We present a novel computational approach that automatically discovers meaningful behavioral patterns in infant-object interactions without requiring predefined labels or linguistic descriptions. Our method analyzes video data of infants playing with toys and uncovers subtle but important behavioral concepts—like different ways of grasping, exploring, or manipulating objects—that traditional analysis methods might miss.

Through extensive experiments on real infant behavior datasets, we demonstrate that our approach can identify previously unknown patterns in how infants learn and interact with their environment. These discoveries provide new insights into early cognitive development and open exciting possibilities for understanding infant learning mechanisms.

🔬 Our Research Question

When a multimodality model, trained on infant daily experience, will its vision encoder develop representations beyond its linguistic input, like real infants?

In this paper, we investigated such an infant model[1]

Wai Keen Vong et al. "Grounded language acquisition through the eyes and ears of a single child." Science 383, 504-511 (2024). DOI:10.1126/science.adi1374
based on cognitive science that infants develop visual understanding earlier than language acquisition. We believe similar phenomenon will also happen in this computational model.

Methodology

1. Discover Visual Concepts from Visual Representations

We leverage CLIP-Dissect[2]

Oikarinen, Tuomas, and Tsui-Wei Weng. "CLIP-Dissect: Automatic Description of Neuron Representations in Deep Vision Networks." ICLR 2023 Spotlight.
to 'label' each neuron with corresponding semantic concepts.

Discovered visual concepts from visual representation are categorized as in-vocabulary or out-of-vocabulary.

Many out-of-vocabulary visual concepts emerge in specific neurons.

We rate visual concepts from a cognitive perspective using Age of Acquisition (AoA)[3]

Kuperman, V., Stadthagen-Gonzalez, H., & Brysbaert, M. (2012). Age-of-acquisition ratings for 30,000 English words. Behavior research methods, 44, 978-990.
.

3. Representation Difference Analysis

Net Dissect Analysis

CKA Matrix Analysis

Our analysis reveals that general representations (CLIP, ResNet) show significant differences in the last few layers (abstract concepts), while sharing similar representations in lower layers (simple patterns). This suggests that high-level visual understanding develops differently across models, while basic visual processing remains consistent.
